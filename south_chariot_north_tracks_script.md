# South Chariot, North Tracks - A Chinese Idiom Story

## Video Script for International Audience

---

### **Opening Scene (0:00 - 0:15)**
**Visual:** Animation of a chariot heading north while a compass points south. Title appears: "South Chariot, North Tracks - A 2,000-Year-Old Chinese Wisdom"

**Narrator (Friendly, engaging tone):**
"Imagine wanting to go south, but pointing your chariot north. Sounds ridiculous, right? But this is exactly the story behind one of China's most famous idioms: '南辕北辙' (Nán Yuán Běi Zhé)."

---

### **The Ancient Story (0:15 - 1:30)**
**Visual:** Reenactment or animation of ancient China, a wealthy man preparing for a journey.

**Narrator:**
"This story comes from the Warring States period, over 2,200 years ago. There was a wealthy man in the state of Wei who wanted to travel to the state of Chu in the SOUTH."

**Visual:** Map showing Wei (north) and Chu (south). The man's chariot is pointing north.

**Narrator:**
"But instead of heading south, he told his driver to go NORTH! A friend saw him and asked: 'You're going to Chu, which is south. Why are you driving north?'"

**Visual:** Conversation between the man and his friend.

**Man (confident, stubborn):**
"My horse is the fastest!"

**Narrator:**
"His friend said: 'But you're going the wrong way!'"

**Man:**
"I have plenty of money for the journey!"

**Narrator:**
"Still, his friend warned him."

**Man:**
"My driver is the most skilled!"

**Visual:** The chariot drives further north, getting farther from Chu.

**Narrator:**
"And so he drove on—faster horse, more money, better driver—all taking him further AWAY from his destination."

---

### **What It Means Today (1:30 - 2:15)**
**Visual:** Modern examples:
- Someone wanting to save money but buying expensive things
- Someone wanting to get fit but eating junk food
- A company wanting more customers but ignoring feedback

**Narrator:**
"Today, 'South Chariot, North Tracks' means: **Your actions are completely opposite to your goals.** It doesn't matter how hard you try, how many resources you have—if you're going the wrong direction, you'll never get where you want to be."

---

### **Why This Matters (2:15 - 2:45)**
**Visual:** Montage of people reflecting, checking maps, adjusting course.

**Narrator:**
"This ancient wisdom reminds us: Before you start moving fast, make sure you're moving in the RIGHT direction. Sometimes, stopping to check your compass is more important than speeding ahead."

---

### **Closing (2:45 - 3:00)**
**Visual:** The idiom characters appear on screen, with English translation.

**Narrator:**
"Remember: The fastest way to get somewhere is to first make sure you're facing the right way. Thanks for watching this Chinese idiom story!"

**On-screen text:**
"南辕北辙 (Nán Yuán Běi Zhé)
South Chariot, North Tracks
Actions opposite to goals"

---

## Production Notes:
- **Tone:** Educational but engaging, like a TED-Ed video
- **Length:** 3 minutes
- **Style:** Mix of animation and modern examples
- **Key takeaway:** Direction matters more than speed
